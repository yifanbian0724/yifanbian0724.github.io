How this list is made

An AI assistant (Claude) runs literature searches for me across PubMed, arXiv and journal websites, looking for new work on facial expression and emotion and for useful methods from nearby fields. I mark each candidate as relevant, maybe or not relevant.

Only the papers I mark relevant are listed here and in the feed. Candidates I have not judged yet are counted under each month but not named. Topics use the names of the topic groups in my library’s galaxy.

The one-line summaries are written for this page with AI, from each paper’s abstract. They are not the authors’ words, and they leave out most of the detail, so read the paper itself before citing it.

How the new papers are placed in the galaxy

The galaxy is a map of my research library: documents sit near documents whose full text covers similar ground. A paper that is not in my library yet has no full text in that map, so it cannot get an exact place, and its star is an approximation.

Each new paper is compared, by its title and abstract, with every library document’s title and most distinctive terms. It is put at the centre of its closest matches, weighted by how close they are, inside the topic group most of them share; the faint lines lead to those matches. Its topic on this page comes from the same step. A paper that is already in my library is ringed at its own place instead.
