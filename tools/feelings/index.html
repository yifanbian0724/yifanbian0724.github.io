Find the word for a feeling

A gentle vocabulary for naming how you feel, from Self-Discovery, an app in development. Tap a petal to open a family of feelings, laid out by how long each one usually lasts and how strong it usually is. Every word has a plain meaning, a close word to compare it with, and a small clay character that moves the way the feeling feels.

    About these words

    A way to name feelings, not a test

    These words are for describing how you feel, not for diagnosing anything. Real moments often mix, fall between words or fit none, and any feeling can be milder or stronger than where it usually sits.

    The families are a way to browse, drawing on several research traditions: families of basic emotions, appraisal theory, and the map of feelings by pleasantness and energy. The flower's layout is a design choice, not a measured map.

    How long a feeling usually lasts, and how strong it usually is, are rough guides. The lengths follow research on how long everyday emotions last, and words that research did not measure are placed by analogy. How long yours lasts depends most on how much it matters to you and how much you keep going back over it.

    Where the words come from

    Several words, and several of the comparisons between close words, were added to Self-Discovery after reading Brené Brown's Atlas of the Heart (Random House, 2021).
